Street Tally

Questions and answers

For the Woodbine entrance doors campaign.

About Street Tally

What is Street Tally?

A quick way to check places in your neighborhood and put what you find on the map. Your organizer picks the places and the questions. You walk over, look, and answer. For this campaign, you check the doors at building entrances around Woodbine Food Hall.

What is OpenStreetMap?

A free map of the world that anyone can use and improve, a bit like Wikipedia for maps. Many apps and websites use it. Your answers go there.

Do I need an account?

No. Just start checking entrances. Your answers are saved and counted as you go. Local mappers check them and add them to the map.

Who puts my answers on the map?

Every change to OpenStreetMap is made under the account of the person who makes it. Local OpenStreetMap mappers look at each answer and, if it makes sense, add it to the map under their own account. When one is on the map, your answers show “✓ on the map”. Already use OpenStreetMap? Tap “I already use OpenStreetMap” and sign in. Your answers then go straight on the map under your username, including the ones you already saved on this phone.

Privacy

Will my answers be public?

Local mappers see your answers without your name or anything that identifies you. Once a mapper adds an answer to OpenStreetMap, it's public like every map change, under the mapper's username. If you signed in with OpenStreetMap, your answers go on the map under your own username.

Is Street Tally tracking me?

No. Your phone uses your location to find the nearest entrances, and it stays on your phone. The one exception: if you report that an entrance has moved and show where, that one spot is saved so it can be fixed on the map. The map pictures load for the area on your screen, like in any map app.

What does Street Tally keep about me?

Your answers and which entrances you checked, so nobody checks the same one twice and your organizer can see progress. It doesn't ask for your name or email. Your answers are kept until local mappers have checked them, and they always count in the campaign's totals.

Can I delete my data?

Yes. In Street Tally, go to Settings, then Account, then Delete my data. That removes your saved answers that aren't on the map yet, including any still waiting on your phone. Answers already on the map stay there, because they're part of OpenStreetMap now. Deleting the app doesn't remove them. Use Delete my data instead. If you signed in with OpenStreetMap, you can also delete that account on openstreetmap.org. Your changes stay on the map, but your username is removed from them. More in our privacy page.

How do I sign out?

If you signed in with OpenStreetMap, tap Sign out on the Campaigns screen.

Answering

Do I have to check them all?

No. Check the ones near you; the app shows the nearest first. Others can do the rest, and the campaign stays open after the event.

What if I'm not sure?

Tap “I'm not sure”. That's fine. Nothing changes on the map for that question.

What if I got one wrong?

Don't worry. Local mappers check every answer before it goes on the map, and anything on OpenStreetMap can be corrected. Tell your organizer which entrance it was.

The entrance isn't there, has moved, or is something else.

Tap “Something's wrong here”, then “It's not here”, “It's moved”, or what you found. Nothing on the map changes, and the organizer gets the report.

I can't get to an entrance.

Tap Skip. Someone else can check it. Don't go into private or staff-only areas.

It says someone already checked it.

Thanks anyway. Go to the next nearest one. The map shows which are still to check.

I have no signal.

Keep going. Your answers are kept on this phone and send by themselves when you have signal, so keep Street Tally installed until then. If you opened the campaign before, you can still see its list. Some may be done already.

It says an answer wasn't needed.

The place changed on the map before your answer was sent, so it didn't need it any more. Nothing for you to do.

The questions

How does this door open?

Look only at the door at this entrance.

Does it open by itself?

A propped-open door doesn't count: if you can't see it open on its own or a button for it, tap “I'm not sure”.

There's no door, just an opening or a gate.

Tap “Something's wrong here”, then “There's no door”.

These answers tell people with wheelchairs, strollers or walkers what to expect at the door. They don't say whether the whole entrance is step-free.

What happens next

What happens to my answers?

They count in the campaign's tally straight away. Local OpenStreetMap mappers then look at each answer and, if it makes sense, add it to the map under their own account.

Who runs this?
